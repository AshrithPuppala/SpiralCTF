Description:
The Spiral Observatory spent eleven winters teaching a machine to remember how the sky was arranged before the stars were scattered. Its engineers called the process corruption: a long, patient drowning of order in noise, tuned by a schedule they were very proud of. When the funding ran out, they sealed the finished model behind a bridge key and left the calibration notes on the desk.
The night-watchman swears the key stopped working after someone "fixed" the schedule. The archive's checksum disagrees with every reconstruction anyone has tried. The weights, naturally, say nothing. Several people have read them closely and come away with a very confident answer.
Nobody mentions the old logbook entry: "We never noised anything. We only ever moved it, in straight lines, from where it was to where it ended up."
The sky is not lost. It is merely further along than you remember.
